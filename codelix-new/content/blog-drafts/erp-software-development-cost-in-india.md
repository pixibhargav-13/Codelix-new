## The six factors that move the price

Two companies can both say "we need an ERP" and end up ten times apart in cost. These are the reasons:

1. Number of modules. Inventory alone is a small project; sales, purchase, inventory, production, accounts and HR together is a large one.
2. Users and roles. Owners, accountants, store staff and salespeople each need different screens and permissions.
3. Process complexity. Standard buy-and-sell flows are quick. Batch tracking, job work, multi-level BOMs or approval chains add time.
4. Integrations. Tally sync, GST e-invoicing, payment gateways, barcode scanners and e-commerce stores each need building and testing.
5. Data migration. Moving years of item masters, ledgers and open orders from Tally or Excel takes real effort to clean and verify.
6. Branches and locations. Multiple warehouses, plants or companies need stock transfers, consolidated reports and access control.

## Custom ERP vs off-the-shelf ERP pricing

Off-the-shelf ERPs look cheaper because they charge per user per month. Over three to five years, those subscriptions often add up to more than a one-time custom setup plus AMC — especially as user count grows. A custom ERP also fits your process instead of forcing your team to work around the software.

| Option | Upfront cost | Ongoing cost | Fits your process |
|---|---|---|---|
| Off-the-shelf ERP | Low | Per-user subscription, rises with team size | Partly — you adapt to it |
| Custom ERP | Higher | AMC of 15–20% of setup | Fully — built around you |
| Tally plus spreadsheets | Very low | Staff time and errors | Poorly once you grow |

## How long ERP development takes

A focused ERP with two or three modules typically goes live in 6–10 weeks. A full ERP covering production, accounts and multiple locations usually takes 3–6 months, rolled out module by module so operations never stop. The biggest variable is not coding — it is how quickly your team can confirm processes and clean up the data being migrated.

## How to keep ERP cost under control

- Start with the module that hurts most, usually inventory or sales, and add the rest after it is in daily use.
- Keep Tally for accounts at first and sync to it, rather than replacing everything on day one.
- Write down your current process before the first meeting; vague requirements are the main cause of overruns.
- Ask for a fixed-price quote per phase, with what is and isn't included in writing.
- Check who owns the code and data. You should, in full.

## Hidden costs to ask about

The setup price is rarely the whole bill. Before you sign, ask any vendor about hosting, GST Suvidha Provider charges for e-invoices, SMS and WhatsApp message costs, paid third-party licences, training for new staff, and the hourly rate for changes after go-live. A good proposal lists all of these up front.

## Frequently asked questions

### What is the minimum cost of ERP software development in India?

A focused setup with a few modules starts around ₹2.5 lakh for our 4bitx ERP. A fully custom build starts higher, from about $10,000 on our published pricing.

### Is there a yearly cost after the ERP goes live?

Yes. Plan for an annual maintenance contract of 15–20% of the setup cost, covering support, GST changes and fixes, plus hosting and any API fees.

### Can a custom ERP work with Tally?

Yes. Many businesses keep Tally for accounting and sync vouchers and ledgers from the ERP, so the accounts team doesn't have to change tools.

### How long does it take to build an ERP?

Two or three modules usually take 6–10 weeks. A company-wide ERP is typically rolled out over 3–6 months, one module at a time.

### Is a custom ERP cheaper than an off-the-shelf ERP?

Upfront, no. Over three to five years it often is, because there are no per-user subscriptions and you only pay for the modules you use.
